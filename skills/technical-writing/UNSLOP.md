# Unslop

Scan the draft for each pattern below, then rewrite what matches. Keep the meaning and the tone.

## Content

1. **Superficial -ing phrases.** "highlighting...", "ensuring...", "reflecting...", "showcasing...", "fostering...". Delete them, or say the fact they gesture at.
2. **Vague attributions.** "Experts believe", "Industry reports suggest". Name the source or delete the claim.
3. **Say what it does, not how it feels.** "SQL you can read" and "types that follow your schema" name a feeling. Name the mechanism or a number instead: "`.toSQL()` returns the exact string sent to the database", "a column rename fails the build". A sentence that could appear unchanged in another project's docs says nothing about this one. Cut it.
4. **Generic conclusions.** "The future looks bright." State the plan or the fact.

## Words

5. **AI vocabulary.** Additionally, crucial, delve, enduring, enhance, fostering, garner, interplay, intricate, landscape, pivotal, robust, seamless, showcase, tapestry, testament, underscore, vibrant. Use the plain word.
6. **Fancy ways to say "is".** "serves as", "stands as", "boasts", "features". Say "is" or "has".
7. **Abstract metaphor nouns.** Substrate, wedge, vector, locus, vantage, nexus, primitive as a noun, harness or scaffolding as a metaphor, surface as in "API surface", bedrock, modality, paradigm, gold-plating, ratchet, evacuate, endgame, north star, flywheel. Pick the concrete word.
8. **Mannered prose.** Aphorisms ("wire it or delete it"), rhetorical fragments, personified code ("the plan holds it"), figurative verbs ("rides along", "stands on"). Say it literally: "a dial worth turning" is "a parameter worth varying".
9. **Adverbs propping up a weak verb.** "runs quickly" is "is fast" or the number. "significantly improves" is the measured change.
10. **Synonym cycling.** Three names for one thing in a paragraph. Pick one and repeat it.

## Structure

11. **"Not just X, but Y."** State the point directly.
12. **Rule of three.** Ideas forced into groups of three. Use the natural number.
13. **False ranges.** "from X to Y" where X and Y are not on one scale. List the items.
14. **Over-compression.** Dropped articles, verbless fragments, arrows, and abbreviations the reader has to decode. "Parser rejects bad date → exit 2, no write" is "The parser rejects a bad date, exits with code 2, and writes nothing."

## Hedging and filler

15. **Filler phrases.** "In order to" is "to". "Due to the fact that" is "because". "It is important to note that" is nothing.
16. **Stacked hedges.** "could potentially possibly be argued that it might" is "may".

## Formatting

17. **Bold everywhere.** Bold a term where it is defined or a label a template fixes, not every name.
18. **Inline-header lists.** A bold label and colon that restate the line: "**Performance:** Performance improved...". Turn it into prose. A bold lead-in that names the item and is followed by new detail is fine.
19. **Decorative emojis** in headings and bullets. Remove them.
20. **Curly quotes.** Use straight quotes.

## Chat habits

21. **Chatbot phrases.** "I hope this helps!", "Let me know if...", "Certainly!", "Found the smoking gun!". Remove them.
22. **Flattery.** "Great question! You're absolutely right!". Answer directly.
